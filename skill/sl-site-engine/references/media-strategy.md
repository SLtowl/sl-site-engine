# Media strategy

Use this reference before building visual directions that may depend on photography, illustration, 3D, video, screenshots, or isolated subject assets.

## Start with the subject

Choose one of four strategies:

1. **Provided** — use the user's approved brand and product assets.
2. **Generated kit** — create the missing subject/graphic assets needed by the concept: photography, illustration, texture or pattern. One to three may suffice, but do not impose a fixed quota.
3. **Image-free** — let type, data, workflow, or product UI carry the concept.
4. **Mixed** — combine provided and generated assets only when their provenance and visual roles remain clear.

Do not treat generated media as a mandatory quality layer. A dashboard, workflow, or abstract service may be strongest without photography. Conversely, do not approximate a fashion model, physical product, place, artwork, or character with crude CSS when a real asset is central to the idea.

## Build a coherent kit

For a large direction set, prefer a reusable kit:

- one anchor asset that establishes subject identity;
- one environmental or contextual asset when the next section needs place or use;
- one detail or alternate crop when material or evidence matters.

Reuse the kit where it supports genuinely different concepts. Generate a distinct asset when the direction needs a different photographic, illustrative or material language. Economy must not collapse the set into the same picture/layout. A proposed brand can combine subject photography with a separate expressive graphic vocabulary; do not wait for a pre-existing brand book to permit illustration.

When identity matters, create the anchor first and use it as a reference for later generations. Reject drift in shape, clothing, materials, markings, lighting logic, or scale.

## Keep responsibility clear

Media owns the subject appearance. Code owns:

- layout and responsive bounds;
- typography and all readable text;
- crop, mask, blend, and layer order;
- interface controls and state;
- transitions and scroll behavior;
- accessibility text and loading behavior.

For implemented references, never bake a complete website, headline, button, chart, or control into generated imagery. A separately labeled exploratory UI sketch is allowed at the approval stage; it is not a final reference or an asset to flatten over the page.

## Quality gate

Reject an asset before layout when it has malformed anatomy or geometry, inconsistent identity, fake embedded text, muddy edges, implausible materials, wrong focal placement, insufficient crop room, or a visual style that fights the intended page.

After layout, reject the composition when the asset looks pasted on: mismatched shadows, incompatible background, accidental color temperature, weak focal crop, or scale that breaks the hierarchy.

Judge the visible subject inside the image, not the image box alone. If a product needs more presence, adjust its layout scale/crop or use a better-suited asset while preserving important details. Requested pattern/illustration is a distinct graphic layer: plan its source and quiet text zones; do not substitute an empty field or a border and call that visual richness.

## Provenance

Record the local file, source or generator, prompt or transformation when relevant, license, and any identity reference. Generated demo assets must be labeled as fictional when a real product or claim could otherwise be inferred.
