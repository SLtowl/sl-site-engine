# Visual development and approval

Use when forming, sketching or reviewing a direction. Design the whole: composition and typography with its imagery, not an empty wireframe decorated at the end.

## Recommended, opt-in reference research

When the user has not already accepted or declined, recommend a short visual-reference search to improve the range of compositional ideas for this brief. Explain the benefit plainly, then ask whether to include it. A recommendation, a preselected UI option, choosing Thorough, or silence is not consent. Start only after opt-in. Respect a refusal without repeated persuasion; continue with original ideas or the user's supplied references. If research is unavailable, disclose that limitation and offer the same fallback.

After opt-in, inspect a small, varied set of relevant work on accessible sources such as Dribbble, other design showcases, or live websites. Inspect the actual visual work, not just search-result titles. Distinguish a showcase mockup from a functioning website. Choose examples for relevant visual qualities, not likes or ranking alone. A handful of sources can suffice; neither five sources nor one platform is a fixed requirement.

For each useful reference, record the source and a short visual reading: first focal point, relationship of subject and headline, reading flow toward the action, density/negative space, and the role of imagery or ornament. Identify the principle worth adapting and why it fits this brief. Several recolored examples of the same text/photo split do not establish a range of compositions.

Use those observations to propose original directions, not to copy a site's identity, assets or exact layout, and not to turn the findings into a fixed style catalog. Keep sources attributable; do not assume found images are licensed for reuse. If the actual work could not be viewed, say so rather than claiming a visual analysis. Research informs judgment; it does not prove superiority or replace checking the resulting sketch.

After analysis, give the user a compact choice of suitable graphic treatments, not a generic list of every medium. Each proposed treatment names the observed principle/source, why it fits the project, and how it could be built (for example, a separate generated image with a code-controlled crop). Show its effect in the composition sketches and let the user choose the pairing or request a combination. Distinguish observation from your inference. With research declined or unavailable, label treatments as original proposals or based on supplied references, not as Dribbble findings. Keep this within the composition checkpoint rather than adding another long questionnaire.

## Build an expressive hypothesis

For each concept, decide what owns the viewport, how the eye moves, how type carries the voice, and which media/graphic elements support it. Propose a subject-specific visual vocabulary when brand identity is absent. Treat content-dense, image-led and restrained concepts as equally legitimate possibilities.

Start expressive when the brief is open: a recognizable dominant idea, intentional hierarchy and confident visual character. An explicit quiet/strict brief takes precedence. More layers, motion or decoration do not automatically make a concept expressive or better.

A small asset kit can include a photograph plus a separate drawn vignette or pattern. These are examples, not mandatory layers. Use separate generation when it materially improves a raster asset; use HTML/CSS/SVG for genuine code-native structure and typography. Avoid forcing everything into either code or an image.

## Link the visual-system decisions

### First-screen content and action map

For a website opening, compose a usable entry point, not only a promotional poster. Before the sketch, place the brand, a concrete offer, useful supporting information, navigation and the next actions into the intended reading order. Use the actual brief to name destinations; when only the hero is in scope, record them as proposed destinations rather than inventing completed pages or forms. For an explicitly isolated section, sparse gallery or single-action screen, keep that scope instead of adding a site header and extra CTAs automatically.

Propose this content/action map to the user as a short list with each block's purpose; agree on it before detailing. An empty side area is not permission to invent or silently add benefits, service terms or marketing copy. Already supplied/approved content needs no new confirmation. Explicit delegation permits a proposed map, not fabricated business facts.

When a commercial brief has separate browsing and enquiry needs, give them distinct actions with a clear primary/secondary hierarchy. Repeating “Contact” three times does not provide three useful choices. A header action may repeat the main action for access, but it does not replace the alternative path. Make navigation distinguishable from captions, with legible labels and intentional placement; its presence in the markup alone is not a visual pass.

Use supporting areas to answer a relevant visitor question from supplied facts: what can be chosen, how customisation works, or another known practical detail. If facts are missing, clarify or propose the content slot without inventing prices, guarantees, lead times, delivery coverage or testimonials. Site destinations and content structure can be proposed; business claims cannot be fabricated.

For “empty left/right” feedback, preserve the accepted focal composition and inspect the side fields together. Strengthen useful content, scale, grouping and action placement where needed; equal text blocks on both sides are not required. Negative space can frame the subject. Repeated slogans, extra buttons with the same purpose, ornamental dots and repeated motifs are not substitutes for missing information. Verify both informative richness and visual balance in the next render.

### Connected visual choices

Record a short design contract before the sketch, then inspect it in the actual render:

- **Subject scale:** identify the intended dominant object and its visible size, not just the image element's rectangle. A large photo with a small object still reads small. Keep subject detail legible at the requested viewport and normal viewing scale; avoid shrinking the product merely to fit oversized type or equal margins. For “too small” feedback, compare the actual object's silhouette/crop before and after at the same viewport. There is no universal hero percentage.
- **Background language:** choose plain, pattern, illustration, texture or an intentional combination with the user’s desired richness. When pattern/illustration is requested, include a recognizable subject-related graphic layer; a perimeter border, barely visible noise or empty space is not that layer. Keep quiet zones behind reading/action areas. For a restrained brief, a well-resolved plain field remains valid.
- **Shape family:** coordinate image masks, controls and containers. An arched photograph does not mathematically require pill buttons or identical radii; coherent soft controls or a deliberate contrast are design choices. When rounded controls are requested, implement actual button rounding. Do not silently fall back to square library defaults.
- **Action palette:** derive primary/secondary/focus colors from the proposed or supplied brand palette and check contrast. A photo's warm lighting or a component's stock accent is not automatic permission to make the CTA orange. Honor color exclusions across all visible controls. Record why the chosen action color belongs to this concept; no hue is a universal default or ban.
- **Icon role:** text-only CTAs are complete when that is the chosen or proposed treatment. For a requested label-plus-icon treatment, pair readable action text with a recognisable subject/action icon; implement that choice instead of defending a bare button. Keep icon family, optical size, alignment and spacing coherent across relevant controls. Distinguish action icons from the brand logo; a logo on every button is not a default. External-link-looking arrows on internal actions need a real meaning. Explicit arrow rejection applies to all relevant controls, including secondary/header actions, without banning other meaningful icons.
- **Component identity:** when a library was selected, record the actual source component and its palette, radius, label/icon-slot and state adaptations. Keep the requested shape and icon treatment after adaptation. Inspect every primary, secondary and header control at readable scale. If the selected library or suitable component cannot be accessed, disclose the missing layer and offer a provisional substitute for approval; do not silently hand-author a lookalike or claim plugin use. No chosen library means no compulsory plugin dependency.

These decisions form one system, not five unrelated style presets. Do not translate a single project's feedback into compulsory patterns, giant product images, rounded buttons or a fixed palette for every website.

## Sketch checkpoint

For one final reference with no agreed composition, show 2–3 quick visual composition sketches before detailed execution. Keep the same brief/content and viewport; vary the dominant subject, subject/headline relationship and reading flow, not just palette or radii. Each option needs a visible sketch, one sentence on its idea/tradeoff and its proposed graphic treatment. A text-only list of layouts is not a visual choice. The user selects the overall composition; the agent may recommend one but does not silently select for them.

For a requested multi-direction set, use its distinct sketches as the choice set; do not automatically create three subvariants for each direction or reduce the requested count. An explicit single-option limit or delegated composition selection takes precedence over the default 2–3. If the composition is already approved, continue from it instead of reopening composition selection; density may still be unresolved.

A sketch must convey composition, type character, graphic language and atmosphere, not merely boxes. Choose the lightest useful method: a rough browser composition or generated visual sketch. Keep its scope to the requested section(s) and defer expensive asset polish until selection. Check that the idea has a credible implementation route before offering it.

- Label it **Sketch — direction approval pending**.
- Generated UI sketches are exploratory only. Explain that text/fonts/details may change during implementation; do not promise exact translation.
- For a coded sketch, the screenshot reflects that current code, but its direction is still unapproved.
- Ask for approval of the direction(s), not another complete questionnaire. Preserve accepted features when revising.
- After approval, realize the accepted composition with exact assets/fonts and browser rendering. If a material feature cannot be implemented, disclose the change and agree on a substitute rather than silently simplifying.

Explicit user delegation can bypass the pause; record it as delegation, not approval. For automated tests, state which choices were prefilled or delegated and which dialogue stages were not exercised.

### Selected-direction checkpoint

Before detailed execution, show one compact decision board:

- **Content:** proposed blocks and distinct actions, each with a short purpose. Ask the user to confirm or adjust; label unknown business details as gaps.
- **Density:** two visible sketches of the selected composition, labelled Calmer and Richer. Keep the same offer, content map, palette, subject and viewport; vary spacing, grouping and integrated graphic richness. Calmer is still expressive and complete; richer does not mean duplicate patterns, extra filler or more CTAs. Ask which density to develop. Two names or verbal descriptions are not two sketches.
- **Connected-library controls:** inspect the real library and select 2–3 suitable button variants yourself, showing actual rendered samples adapted to the agreed palette/shape and intended action. Recommend one with a short reason; ask for a choice without making the user search the catalog. Pick other relevant elements for their actual function, not as decoration. Record source IDs and adaptations. If fewer suitable variants exist, show the available ones and explain; never invent components or count recolors as distinct variants. If source is unavailable, disclose it instead of fabricating previews or provenance. No selected library means no compulsory library or comparison.

Combine confirmation of content, density and controls into this board rather than three new questionnaires. Already approved fields are fixed; show only unresolved choices. A composition approval alone is not a density choice. Explicit delegated choices or a hard output limit take precedence; state proposed decisions rather than inventing approval. A local correction does not reopen this checkpoint. For a large direction set, apply density exploration to the direction selected for development, not automatically to every member of the set. If the user wants the set finalized without selection, ask once about shared density preference or accept explicit delegation; do not silently double the requested outputs.

## Effort modes

**Quick:** prepare the first candidate set, perform the required checks and show it for feedback. Fix actual rendering/correctness defects; do not secretly turn it into a long search for the ideal design.

**Thorough:** add one bounded internal alternative/selection/revision cycle to improve the candidate set; this internal selection does not replace the user's composition choice. Concentrate alternatives on unresolved or weak ideas rather than doubling every asset automatically. Preserve approved directions. The user-facing count does not change. Further material expansion needs agreement; do not promise exact time or generation cost before tool conditions are known.

Both modes retain the sketch checkpoint unless explicitly delegated, and identical truth, preference and fidelity requirements. Do not show broken work as final to satisfy a speed promise.

## Positive visual review

Treat faithful implementation as the final-reference acceptance gate: do not average a code mismatch away with high aesthetic scores. Among candidates that meet that gate, review originality, beauty and clarity of the offer together. The visitor should understand what is offered and the intended next action; novelty must not obscure that message. These are judgment criteria, not a numeric beauty guarantee.

Inspect the actual image at full composition and readable detail scale:

1. **Composition:** a deliberate focal point and reading sequence; inspect the actual visible subject's scale against type and whitespace, not only its container. Space and density support the subject instead of feeling unfinished or crowded.
2. **Typography:** expressive but legible roles, coherent scale and spacing, natural line breaks; an exact font is not enough by itself.
3. **Graphic language:** background richness, imagery, control shape, action colors and icons satisfy the recorded visual system and preferences. Texture or illustration is integrated rather than pasted on. Restraint is allowed when requested or proposed, not a substitute for requested ornament.
4. **Voice:** heading/support/action form a natural, meaningful message appropriate to the brand; no awkward literal list masquerading as copywriting.
5. **Distinctness:** the concept offers a recognizable idea for this brief. For sets, compare actual compositions and communication approaches, not just palette or font swaps.
6. **Website completeness:** for a site opening, check the visible brand, useful supporting content, recognisable navigation and action hierarchy against the action map. Evaluate what the visitor can understand and choose, not a count of buttons or filled pixels. Check requested icons and component provenance, not just button color. Respect an explicitly sparse or single-action brief.

If blandness is the problem, revisit the dominant idea, hierarchy, type or visual vocabulary. Adding random detail and removing all detail are not repair recipes. Record a concrete weakness and the decision addressing it. An internal self-review remains subjective; the user judges whether the direction works for them.

## Feedback verification

Choose revision scope from what was actually rejected:

- **Local feedback:** preserve composition, copy, assets and accepted choices outside the named layer. Change its necessary dependencies only (such as button text contrast after a fill change), and disclose them. Do not use a button correction as permission to redesign the hero.
- **Whole-direction rejection:** retain factual brief constraints and explicit preferences, return to the composition checkpoint, and offer fresh directions rather than repeatedly polishing the rejected layout.
- **Ambiguous feedback:** ask one focused scope question or propose the smallest reversible correction; do not infer permission for a complete redesign.

If a local request cannot be satisfied without a material layout change, explain the conflict and ask before expanding it.

For a revision, keep a compact request → changed layer → screenshot evidence record. Reuse supplied choices rather than asking the same question again. Inspect every named control, including header and secondary actions. For scale changes compare equal-size captures and the subject itself; for graphic changes inspect visibility and legibility, not merely file existence. Preserve earlier outputs when requested. A feedback-driven improved draft is not a fresh independent A/B result and cannot by itself prove the skill caused a quality improvement.
