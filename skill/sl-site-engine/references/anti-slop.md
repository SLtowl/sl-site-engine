# Anti-slop and quality review

Read before writing copy/UI and revisit on the rendered screen. Distinguish hard correctness failures, the skill's design exclusions, the current user's taste constraints, and contextual visual warnings. None is an instruction to make every page sparse.

## Hard correctness failures

Block or repair these before calling a reference final:

- invented business evidence: metrics, testimonials, awards, partnerships, capabilities, prices or guarantees presented as real;
- text obscured by effects, unreadable contrast, unintended overflow or broken essential assets/fonts;
- final UI text or controls baked into generated imagery rather than implemented;
- unavailable/unlicensed resources presented as reproducible, or a critical effect with no implementation path;
- controls presented as functional when they are not, misleading success states or inconsistent visible values;
- claiming a selected library was used when its actual components were not;
- a whole-interface generated image presented as a browser render;
- duplicate concepts presented as different directions merely to reach a count.

Clearly labeled fictional concept copy/assets are allowed; labels belong in the handoff, not necessarily over the artwork. Static preview controls may illustrate an intended action without pretending a real order/booking happened.

## Excluded design treatments

Do not generate decorative section/card indices such as `01 /`, `02`, or tiny captions/eyebrows attached to ornamental leading, trailing or underline strokes. Do not offer these as options or reintroduce them because a reference uses them or they supposedly look premium. Replace them with plain meaningful labels, typography, spacing or a relevant graphic treatment. This is a deliberate product constraint, not a claim that all designers must share it.

Scope by function: real prices, dates, quantities, ordered task steps and pagination are information, not decorative numbering. Ordinary link underlines, focus indicators, table borders and meaningful separators are not line-caption ornament. Preserve that functionality. If supplied brand material conflicts with an exclusion, surface the conflict instead of silently editing the brand or offering a decorative opt-in.

## User-configured preferences

Explicit rejections in this brief block those treatments. Preserve supplied approved copy and brand choices unless asked to change them. Do not import another client's or the skill author's preferences.

Examples of configurable choices: plain meaningful eyebrow labels, icon treatment, arrows, pill controls, gradients, illustration/pattern density or particular slogan patterns. These remain contextual; the two excluded treatments above are not preference switches. Real prices, ordered steps, data and useful navigation cues are not decorative numbers.

Configurable does not mean unasked or unchecked: use the briefing checkpoint and the linked visual-system review. Explicit preferences are acceptance conditions in the current task. A style explanation cannot override a rejected arrow, color or shape. Check controls together rather than approving a hero crop while ignoring header buttons.

If no preference is given, assess the treatment in context. Do not silently choose a universal “strict anti-slop” style preset. A text-only action is valid; so is a suitable icon when it supports the intended interaction or agreed visual language.

## Copy and micro-detail review

Read the heading, supporting text and CTA together. Can a visitor understand the offer and next step? Does the voice match the brief? Evocative language can accompany concrete information; literal ingredient/category lists are not inherently better writing.

Interchangeable claims such as “More than…” are warning signs when they displace the offer or clash with the requested tone, not automatic failures in all brands. Check for awkward phrasing, redundant labels, competing microcopy and decorative pseudo-data. Rewrite the actual message, not just a noun in a generic slogan.

Inspect numbers, rules, labels and icons at readable size. Keep useful information and deliberately composed expression; remove accidental or conflicting noise. A named user prohibition cannot be excused by claiming the detail looks premium.

For newly proposed wordmarks and short display headlines, default to no ornamental terminal full stop. Do not append a dot merely to make the brand or slogan look designed. Preserve supplied/approved brand spelling and copy, including intentional punctuation; an explicit user correction takes precedence. This display convention does not remove sentence punctuation, abbreviations, decimal points or punctuation from normal prose. Review the actual wordmark and heading separately from the paragraph text.

An empty-feeling hero needs a content/action review, not just an ornament pass: check whether supporting text adds information beyond the slogan and whether navigation and distinct visitor actions are visible. A reference with requested icons missing or a chosen library silently replaced is not preference-complete. Conversely, do not force dense copy, multiple CTAs or icons into an explicitly restrained, single-action brief.

## Expression is a legitimate visual role

A detail may serve content, hierarchy, action, feedback, existing brand identity, or a **proposed subject-specific expression**. Illustration, pattern, texture and type can establish the identity of a new brand; they do not need a pre-existing brand book or a click action.

Ask what the detail contributes to the composition, mood, recognition or subject, and whether it fits the rest. “Premium” alone is vague; a coherent drawn botanical vocabulary, tactile print treatment or deliberate typographic contrast can be a concrete visual idea. Do not add those examples to every page.

Assess assets with their typography/layout. Temporarily removing an image may diagnose weak composition, but dependence on a central photograph is not itself a failure. Judge the intended finished whole. Check crop, edges, lighting, scale and color integration; a technically valid but visibly pasted-on asset needs repair.

## Contextual warning clusters

Review familiar treatments when they accumulate without a subject-specific idea: equal feature cards, aurora/glow/glass stacks, repeated pills, uniform card styling, generic CTA copy, oversized unsupported headings, identical image placement across concepts or repetitive reveals.

The number of warnings triggers review, not a beauty score or automatic style ban. For manifests, three or more warning signals require a short brief-specific rationale. A rationale is not a substitute for inspecting the image. Pairwise concept keys require actual visual comparison, not synonyms that game a script.

## Review record

Keep a short note tied to the screenshot: exact heading/CTA, preference compliance, factual/technical checks, one concrete visual strength and any weakness addressed or disclosed, asset integration and component provenance. Use [visual-development.md](visual-development.md) for the positive visual checks.

Do not average correctness failures into a total score. Passing checks does not establish that a design is beautiful, better than a baseline, or immune to future AI slop. Respect the user's visual judgment and use criticism to revise the relevant decision rather than adding a universal ban.
